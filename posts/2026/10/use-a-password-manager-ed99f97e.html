How to Choose and Use a Password Manager Safely

Using a different password for every online account helps limit the damage if one service is breached. A password manager can generate and store unique passwords so you do not have to memorize them all. The important part is choosing a reputable tool and protecting access to its vault.

1. Choose a reputable tool

Compare the security features and recovery process before storing important credentials. Look for clear security documentation, regular updates, password generation, multi-factor authentication support, and compatibility with your devices. Download apps and browser extensions only from the provider's official website or a trusted app store.

  • Security: Read how the provider protects stored data and explains its security practices.
  • Recovery: Understand what happens if you lose your master passphrase or main device.
  • Portability: Check whether you can securely export or move your data if you change services.

2. Protect your master passphrase

Your master password or passphrase can unlock many saved accounts. Make it long, unique, and difficult to guess. A passphrase made from several randomly selected words can be memorable if the service supports it. Never reuse a password from your email, social media, or banking accounts. Enable multi-factor authentication for the password manager when available, and never share your master passphrase or recovery codes in messages.

3. Secure your most important accounts first

Start with accounts that could be used to reset other passwords or cause serious harm if compromised:

  1. Your primary email account.
  2. Financial and payment accounts.
  3. Cloud storage and important work accounts.
  4. Social media and messaging accounts.
  5. Other websites and apps.

Replace reused or weak passwords with unique ones. Enable multi-factor authentication wherever possible, especially for your email and password manager accounts.

4. Prepare recovery options

Before an emergency, learn how to regain access if your phone or computer is lost, damaged, or replaced. If the service provides recovery codes, store them securely and separately from the account they protect. If encrypted backups or emergency access are available, understand how they work and follow the provider's official instructions.

5. Watch for phishing and unsafe autofill

A password manager cannot stop every scam. Check website addresses before signing in, especially if a message says your account will be closed or demands urgent action. Open important services through their official app or a verified bookmark. Never reveal your master passphrase or verification codes to someone who contacts you unexpectedly, and do not approve an unexpected multi-factor authentication request.

Quick safety checklist

  • Choose a reputable password manager and install it from an official source.
  • Create a long, unique master passphrase.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication if available.
  • Replace reused passwords on important accounts first.
  • Store recovery codes securely and separately.
  • Keep your devices and password manager updated.
  • Check website addresses and treat unexpected sign-in requests with caution.
