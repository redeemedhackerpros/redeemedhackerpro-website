How to Use a Password Manager to Protect Your Accounts

Reusing the same password across several websites creates a risk: if one service is breached, attackers may try that password on your other accounts. A reputable password manager can help you create and store a unique, strong password for each account.

How to get started

  1. Choose a reputable password manager that fits your needs and devices.
  2. Create a strong, unique master password and protect your recovery method.
  3. Start by updating the password for your primary email account.
  4. Use unique passwords for your financial, social media, and other important accounts.
  5. Enable multifactor authentication wherever it is available, especially for your email and password manager.

Keep your recovery options safe

Store recovery codes somewhere secure and separate from the account they protect. Be cautious of unexpected messages asking you to reveal your master password or authentication codes. A password manager can reduce password-reuse risks, but it does not replace software updates, phishing awareness, or multifactor authentication.
